Biology · 2023

Polyherbal and Multimodal Treatments: Kaempferol- and Quercetin-Rich Herbs Alleviate Symptoms of Alzheimer’s Disease

C. Alexander, A. Parsaee, M. Vasefi

Read the paper ↗

Alzheimer’s disease has many interacting causes, so a treatment that acts on several of them at once may help more than a single-target drug.

Map of the connections described in the review.

The problem

Alzheimer’s disease involves plaques, tangles, inflammation, oxidative stress and damaged neuroplasticity. Current treatments are limited, and the evidence on herbal alternatives was scattered across many small studies.

What we did

We followed PRISMA guidelines to identify herbal treatments, their active compounds and their likely mechanisms, then organized the findings into one mechanistic picture.

What we found

  • Common herbs, including Ginkgo biloba, Camellia sinensis, Glycyrrhiza uralensis, Cyperus rotundus and Bupleurum falcatum, gave promising pre-clinical results.
  • These herbs are rich in the flavonoids kaempferol and quercetin, which act on several mechanisms at once.
  • We proposed that a kaempferol- and quercetin-rich herbal mix could treat Alzheimer’s-related brain damage.
Go deeper

The evidence for kaempferol was much thinner than for quercetin, so its proposed mechanisms rest partly on what is known about quercetin. That is a hypothesis for future studies, not an established result.
