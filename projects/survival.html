Budget-constrained active learning

Which patients should we follow, and for how long?

Under review · ICLR 2027 · arXiv 2025

Budget-constrained Active Learning to Effectively De-censor Survival Data

Ali Parsaee, Bei Jiang, Zachary Friggstad, Russell Greiner
MSc thesis work, University of Alberta

Read the paper (arXiv) ↗

When follow-up is expensive, spend it on the patients and durations that teach the model the most per unit of cost, even though a follow-up may end in “not yet”.

Simplified illustration on simulated patients. Here “information” is about each patient’s own event time; in the paper it is information about the model, as in BatchBALD.

The problem

Survival data contain censored patients: we only know they were event-free up to some time. Following them up costs time and money, and the answer is often partial. A follow-up can reveal the event, or only move the “event-free until” date. Standard active learning assumes every query returns a complete label.

What we did

We extended budgeted active learning to censored data. A query buys a follow-up of a chosen length for a chosen patient and can return a partial label. The acquisition rule extends BatchBALD to these partial answers and to unequal costs.

What we found

  • Bounds and time complexity asymptotically equivalent to BatchBALD, the standard batch active learning method.
  • Across several real survival datasets, the method performed better than the other approaches we compared.
Go deeper

A censored patient (3 years, censored) can be probed for one more year. The result is either an event at, say, 3.2 years, or a new censoring time of 4 years. The learner scores every patient and probe depth by the expected information it buys, divided by its cost, and spends the budget greedily. Choosing batches this way is a maximum-coverage problem, which is why greedy selection comes with an approximation guarantee.
