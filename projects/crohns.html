Predicting bowel resection in Crohn’s disease

Useful prediction from incomplete clinical observations.

Journal of Pediatric Surgery · 2026

Machine learning predicts first elective bowel resection in newly diagnosed pediatric Crohn disease patients

Ricardo G. Suarez Suarez, Ali Parsaee, Shahzaib Ahmed, … Russell Greiner, Eytan Wine, and the Canadian Children IBD Network
Second of 22 authors

Read the paper ↗

Instead of one risk score, each child gets a whole curve: the probability of a first bowel resection at every future time.

Illustrative curves, not the paper’s fitted model. The error figures are the paper’s.

The problem

Children with Crohn disease need surgery more often than adults. Existing risk scores do not give an individual child’s probability of surgery over time.

What we did

We learned models that predict individual survival distributions from clinical and laboratory data in a prospective national cohort of 934 children. Two SuperLearners used baseline data alone, or baseline plus week-10 data, each selecting among survival models by cross-validation.

What we found

  • Predicted surgery times were off by 755 days on average with baseline data and 840 days with week-10 data added.
  • The features the models selected matched earlier clinical studies: penetrating and stricturing disease, and the perianal modifier.
Go deeper

Many children had not had surgery by the end of follow-up, so their outcomes are censored. Individual survival distributions use those children too, rather than discarding them.
