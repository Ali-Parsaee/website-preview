LoopBench

What can emerge from a swarm’s interactions?

Preprint · arXiv 2025

LoopBench: Discovering Emergent Symmetry Breaking Strategies with LLM Swarms

Ali Parsaee, Yashar Talebirad, Csongor Szepesvári, Vishwajeet Ohal, Eden Redman
First author

Read the paper (arXiv) ↗

Identical agents that each act sensibly can lock into an endless loop together. Escaping it takes an agent that reflects on its own history and changes its rule.

Simulated agents with hand-written rules. Benchmark numbers are from the paper.

The problem

Language models are increasingly used as autonomous agents, but little is known about whether independent agents, each with a local view, can invent the strategies needed to coordinate without a central controller.

What we did

We built a benchmark around colouring odd rings (3, 5 and 11 nodes) with two colours. A perfect colouring is impossible, and simple greedy agents oscillate forever. Each node is an LLM agent that sees its local history and can pass a short note of strategy to its future self.

What we found

  • Advanced reasoning models (O3) devised strategies to escape the deadlock. On the 5-node ring O3 scored 57.5% proximity to the best colouring with 100% stability.
  • GPT-4.1 Nano agents scored 4.4% proximity on the same ring.
  • Classical heuristics struggled, revealing a measurable reasoning gap between classes of models.
Go deeper

The benchmark tests meta-cognitive reasoning: thinking narrowly leads to deadlock, and the solution only appears when an agent steps back and questions its own rule.
