When Does Censor Awareness Help Survival-Policy Identification?

Ali Parsaee and collaborators

When outcomes can stay censored, what matters is how often events are observed among the patients who can tell the candidate policies apart, not the trial’s overall censoring rate.

Illustration of the question on simulated patients. No results from the manuscript are shown.

This manuscript is under review. This page explains the question and the idea; results will be added once the paper is public.

The problem

A trial that must choose a treatment rule observes event times, or only that no event happened before follow-up ended. Pure-exploration theory tells us how to allocate a budget when outcomes arrive immediately, and adaptive survival trials handle censoring when the goal is estimation. Choosing a policy under censoring sits between the two.

What we did

The project studies how to allocate recruitment across patient groups and randomized treatments so that the best policy from a candidate set is identified with as little budget as possible, when outcomes may remain censored.

What we found

  • The results are under review and will be posted when the paper is public.
Go deeper

The target is a final policy recommendation, not every model parameter. Information about a parameter matters only if it changes which policy looks best.
