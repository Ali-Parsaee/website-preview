Learning what to grow

Which experiment buys the most improvement in cell culture?

Bioinformatics · 2026

A data-driven evaluation of optimization techniques in cell culture media

Ali Parsaee, Miranda Stahn, Arian Amirvaresi, Reza Ovissipour, David Staszak
First author

Read the paper ↗Code and data ↗

On real measured cell-culture formulations, Bayesian optimization with a Gaussian process found good media in fewer experiments than classical designs or random search.

Simulated landscape in the spirit of the Hide-the-Label protocol. The finding about Gaussian-process optimization is the paper’s.

The problem

Optimizing a growth medium for cultivated meat is slow and expensive: every formulation has to be made and measured. Earlier comparisons of optimizers relied mostly on simulations, which left labs without evidence on which strategy to adopt.

What we did

We built two benchmark protocols and ran them on six published cell-culture datasets. In “Hide-the-Label”, an optimizer starts with a partial view and reveals hidden formulations one at a time or in batches, like costly lab measurements. In “Open Race”, methods compete under a fixed evaluation budget. We tested four surrogate models (Gaussian process, random forest, neural network, Bayesian neural network), two difficulty regimes, and batch sizes from 1 to 20.

What we found

  • Gaussian-process Bayesian optimization was consistently the most sample-efficient strategy.
  • Its advantage narrowed but remained under added noise and multiple peaks.
  • Optimizers built on more complex surrogates (deeper networks, adaptive ensembles) did worse, not better, on the harder landscapes.
Go deeper

The paper also sets out criteria for choosing benchmark datasets and how to draw statistically robust conclusions when measurements are biased and heterogeneous, ending with a roadmap for choosing a strategy in practice.
