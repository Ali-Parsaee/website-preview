When Is a Learned Treatment Policy Worth Personalizing?

Ali Parsaee, with Tina Sami

A learned treatment rule can beat observed practice only because one treatment for everyone would already beat it. The gain from personalizing is what is left after that.

Simulated outcomes. Sliders change the average benefit of one treatment, how much that benefit varies, and the study size.

This manuscript is under review. This page explains the question and the idea; results will be added once the paper is public.

The problem

Learned treatment policies are usually compared with what clinicians actually did. That comparison mixes two questions: could practice improve by choosing one action for everyone, and does tailoring the action to each patient add anything beyond that?

What we did

The project separates the two gains, evaluates learned policies against the better of the two comparators, and allows the evaluation to withhold a verdict when the evidence or the diagnostics cannot support one.

What we found

  • The results are under review and will be posted when the paper is public.
Go deeper

A finding about one learned rule is not a claim about the best possible personalized policy. The quantity of interest is the improvement of the tested rule over a single rule for everyone.
