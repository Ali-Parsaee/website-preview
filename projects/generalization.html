Generalization versus memorization

When is learning a pattern cheaper than remembering examples?

Research manuscript · under review

Generalization versus memorization: exact costs in small networks

Ali Parsaee
First-author research project

A network that encodes a rule can be far cheaper than one that memorizes examples. Whether training actually finds the cheap solution is a separate question.

Two hand-built networks that both classify parity correctly. Costs are exact for these constructions; they are not the optimum computed in the manuscript.

This manuscript is under review. This page explains the question and the idea; results will be added once the paper is public.

The problem

Networks can fit their training examples long before they generalize. One explanation is that training with weight decay prefers small-weight solutions, and rules tend to need smaller weights than lookup tables. That turns three questions into one: how cheap a good solution can be, whether one exists at a given size, and whether gradient descent reaches it.

What we did

The project computes exact representation costs in small ReLU networks on structured tasks such as parity, then asks separately whether optimization reaches the cheapest solutions.
