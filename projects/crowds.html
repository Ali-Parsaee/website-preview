Wisdom of the Machines

What can a diverse crowd of models know together?

AAMAS 2026 · Paphos, Cyprus

Wisdom of the Machines: Exploring Collective Intelligence in LLM Crowds

Yashar Talebirad*, Ali Parsaee*, Vishwajeet Ohal, Amirhossein Nadiri, Csongor Szepesvári, Yash Mouje, Eden Redman
* equal contribution

Read the paper ↗

A crowd of language models is wise when its members are different models. Sampling one model many times repeats that model’s mistakes.

Simulated crowd: each model has its own bias, and temperature adds noise around it. The 68% figure is from the paper.

The problem

Averaging many independent human guesses often beats any individual: the “wisdom of crowds”. Large language models can produce many guesses cheaply, but it was unclear how to form a useful crowd from them.

What we did

We compared two sources of diversity on vision-based estimation tasks: response diversity, from sampling one model at several temperatures, and model diversity, from asking different models. The datasets were people’s height and weight from photos, small objects with known weights, and Amazon products with their prices.

What we found

  • Aggregating temperature-0 answers from a diverse set of models was the most effective strategy, and it outperformed any single model.
  • Temperature-induced diversity gave no significant benefit while increasing inference cost.
  • The median of deterministic answers from several models beat 68% of individual guesses on average.
  • Context helped where it was directly informative, such as product titles for price estimation.
Go deeper

The median is robust to a few wild answers, which matters because language models occasionally misread an image badly. Model diversity works because different models were trained differently and so err in different directions; their errors partly cancel.
