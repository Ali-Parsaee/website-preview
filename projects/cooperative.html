Cooperative observation

How can agents observe usefully together?

PILA workshop · KDD 2026

Toward Personal Intelligence Through Cooperative Observation

Yashar Talebirad, Osman Jime, Ali Parsaee, Eden Redman, Yongbin Kim, Osmar R. Zaïane

Read the paper (arXiv) ↗

What a personal AI can observe depends on whether its help earns continued access: usefulness, trust and access form one loop.

Illustrative dynamics of the loop, not data.

The problem

A personal AI needs a model of its user’s goals and commitments, and that model is limited by what it can observe. More observation does not help by itself: the system must select and compress, and the user decides what it may see.

What we did

We framed this as cooperative observation: the system acts on a partial model, the user evaluates the result, and that evaluation shapes what the system may observe next. We report a preliminary single-user account from Organizm, a prototype used over six months.

What we found

  • A framework connecting observation quality, usefulness, trust and future access.
  • Evaluation directions for measuring how observation quality shapes personal AI.
  • A six-month single-user deployment record; controlled multi-user studies are needed to test causes.
Go deeper

Useful, inspectable behaviour gives users a reason to keep or widen the channel; failures lead them to correct it, narrow it, revoke it or abandon the system.
