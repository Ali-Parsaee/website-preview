Risk after radiation therapy

Predicting severe adverse events from clinical records.

ASTRO Annual Meeting 2026 · abstract

Development of a Post-Radiation “Safety Net”: A Machine Learning Approach to Predicting Severe Adverse Events Using the SEER Database

A. Parsaee, K. G. Samuel, N. Mohan, P. Wong
First author

Read the abstract ↗

Registry data can train a sensitive screening model that flags most patients at risk of early death after radiation therapy, so they get closer monitoring.

Simulated patients, tuned so the operating point matches the paper’s recall and precision.

The problem

Severe complications after radiation therapy need early attention, and value-based care programs need a way to prioritize monitoring. Early mortality within three months serves as a proxy for severe acute adverse events.

What we did

We analysed 235,035 patients treated with radiation in the US SEER database (2010–2022), using nine patient-reportable variables across medical, socioeconomic and metadata domains. We compared classifiers and Cox survival models, and a global model against site-specific models for lung, breast and prostate cancer.

What we found

  • Early mortality affected 4% of patients. A gradient boosting classifier flagged 92% of them (recall 0.92) at precision 0.10, an acceptable trade-off for a safety net.
  • The global Cox model reached a C-index of 0.80; within individual sites it averaged 0.76, and site-specific models recovered 0.78.
  • Age, income and marital status were significant independent predictors.
Go deeper

Next steps are Statistics Canada data with actual radiation-related adverse events, richer socioeconomic features, and multicentre validation on independent records before clinical use.
