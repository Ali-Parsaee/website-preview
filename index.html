Acquire

Many cheap, noisy sources can be worth more than one expensive one, because their mistakes cancel.

error of the average of n independent guesses ∝ 1 / √nCondorcet, 1785: a crowd of slightly-better-than-chance voters is right more often than any member.

The catch is independence. Agents that copy each other, or were trained the same way, are one source wearing many hats. So the real question is how to buy diverse opinions cheaply, and how to tell when you have.

Hold

Keeping information has a price too. How much detail is worth keeping, as it ages?

precision(t) should fall with age t, so old memories become summaries and recent ones stay sharpA memory is a bet that the detail will be worth more later than the space it takes now.

For an agent with limited memory, this is a hierarchy: exact recent events, compressed older ones, and the rules extracted from both. What an agent keeps also shapes what it can say.

Price

A dataset is worth what it teaches, not how big it is.

worth of an example = the improvement it buys on new cases, not on itselfMemorising a list and learning a rule look the same on the training data. They part ways on the next example.

So the price of information depends on who is learning. The same thousand rows are nearly free to a model that already knows the rule and priceless to one that does not.

Spendflagship

With a fixed budget, which observations and experiments buy the most certainty?

choose the set S with Σ cost(i) ≤ B that maximises information(S)Hard in general. For the right objective, greedy "most information per dollar" is provably within (1 − 1/e) of the best possible.

In medicine many outcomes are censored: we only know the event has not happened yet. That changes the price of everything, because an experiment is only worth paying for if you will live to see its answer.

The value of an experiment depends on how likely its outcome is to be observed, relative to its cost. So the experiments worth running under censoring are not the ones an uncensored analysis would choose.
My master's thesis. It ranks patient–duration choices by expected information per unit of expected cost, with a (1−1/e) guarantee for the batch, by connecting batch active learning to the maximum-coverage problem. Read it on arXiv.

Ask

When an expert is always available, when should a learner ask, and when should it struggle on its own?

ask when E[gain from the answer] > cost of asking + what you stop learning by not tryingThe second term is the one people forget. Deferring is not free: unused skills decay.

This is the newest station and the one I am moving toward. It is also the question every person now has with the models on their desk.

Two agents who only know each other through conversation. Even when one is better at everything, both gain by specializing in what each is relatively best at, and deferring too much has a cost, because skills you stop using decay.
