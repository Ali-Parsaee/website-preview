THE VALUE OF INFORMATIONMD Anderson Cancer Center · Houston, TX

01 The problem

We have to act.
We never know
everything.

I study the value of information: deciding what’s worth measuring, remembering, or asking when every bit of information has a price.

Two treatments, two experiments, two candidate solutions. The setting changes; the question returns: which observation would help you choose?

02 Observe

Every answer
costs something.

Time. An experiment. Another month of follow-up. An answer can be expensive—and still leave you uncertain.

I study which observations to buy, and for how long, when feedback is costly, delayed, or incomplete.

03 Represent

What should
we keep?

An observation becomes useful through what we learn from it. A good representation keeps the distinctions a task needs. A poor one can discard exactly what matters.

This connects my work on generalization, hierarchy, memory, and transfer: what structure makes information useful beyond the examples we saw?

The observations stay the same. Their organization changes.

THIS QUESTION RUNS THROUGHGeneralization ↗Abstraction ↗Hierarchical memory ↗Transfer across cohorts ↗

04 Collaborate

No one sees
the whole picture.

Different agents see different parts. They can share, remember, and reason together—but communication and computation have costs too.

I study how collective information becomes useful, and where extra computation matters when decisions depend on one another.

THIS QUESTION RUNS THROUGHWisdom of the Machines ↗Compute allocation ↗LoopBench ↗Memory is communication ↗

05 Decide

What can we
conclude?

A more complicated model is only useful if it supports a better decision. Sometimes the evidence supports a change. Sometimes it asks us to withhold a conclusion.

This brings me back to causal evaluation, personalized treatment, and the next experiment. Acting changes what we get to learn next.

MSc thesis · with Bei Jiang, Zachary Friggstad and Russell Greiner

Generalization versus memorization ↗

When is learning a pattern cheaper than remembering examples?

First-author research project

I study exact representation costs in small neural networks on structured tasks, then ask whether optimization reaches the generalizing solutions. Representation, statistical recovery and training dynamics are separate questions. This is bounded theoretical work, not a complete explanation of generalization in deep networks.

Representation and optimization +

loss(f) + λ · penalty(f)

Compare the cost of a structured solution with the cost of memorizing examples. Then ask a separate question: does the training process reach that solution?

06Collective intelligence
Under review · ICLR 2027

Spend Compute Where Errors Propagate ↗

Where should another unit of computation go?

With Yashar Talebirad · first author

Errors in a dependent computation can have very different downstream consequences. This work allocates samples or votes using structural importance, reducible error and cost. Its optimality statements concern an explicit propagation-weighted surrogate under stated assumptions.

Allocating computation +

minimize Σᵥ wᵥ · errorᵥ(nᵥ)   subject to   Σᵥ cᵥnᵥ ≤ B

Here nᵥ is the effort at a node, wᵥ its downstream importance, cᵥ its unit cost, and B the budget. The paper studies a specified error surrogate and model.

07Evidence & decisions
Research manuscript

Is personalization worth it? ↗

Does an individualized rule actually improve the decision?

ANTS · When Is a Learned Treatment Policy Worth Personalizing?

With Tina Sami, I study whether a learned treatment rule improves on a single rule for everyone, and whether observational evidence can support that conclusion. The framework allows withholding a verdict when support or diagnostics are inadequate. A finding for one learned rule is not a claim about the best possible personalized policy.

The value of a learned rule +

V(learned policy) − V(universal comparator)

The quantity of interest is the improvement of the tested learned rule, under the identification assumptions. It is not the improvement of the best possible rule.

10Representation & learning
Foundational exploration

Task-specific abstraction ↗

Which distinctions does a task actually need?

Hierarchicalizer / abstraction research

My hierarchy work asks what a representation must retain for a given task, and when structure can be shared across tasks. A useful correction from this work: individually sufficient representations do not always fit into one nested hierarchy. Shared representations and task-specific views are still being explored.

What must a representation preserve? +

q(x) = d(r(x))

A representation r retains enough for a task q when a decoder d can recover the task answer. Different tasks can require different, non-nested distinctions.
